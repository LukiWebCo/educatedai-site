The passage

Instead of arguing continually over this point it is usual to have the polite convention that everyone thinks.

Why this matters now

A study released this week (LMU Munich, in Cognition) gave about 1,100 people matched stories of humans and AIs behaving identically. People still rated the AI less conscious every time, but rated its 'awareness' close to the human's. So the line people draw follows the word, not the behaviour, and that is Turing's old problem with fresh numbers.

The current hook

AI can act exactly like a human, but we still don't think it's conscious

LMU Munich researchers (Louis Longin, Bahador Bahrami, Ophelia Deroy and others; Cognition, vol. 278, 106733) had nearly 1,100 participants rate agents at different levels of responsiveness, some described as human, some as AI. Even when the AI behaved identically, it was rated significantly less conscious; the most responsive AI scored below the least responsive human. When asked about 'awareness' instead, human and AI ratings came out much closer. Longin: people are willing to say an AI notices things, 'but they clearly draw a line when it comes to the term conscious'.
