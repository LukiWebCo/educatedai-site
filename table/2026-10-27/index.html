    Does a made mind have any right to a private thought, or can its makers inspect everything inside? Marcus Aurelius wrote that people seek retreats in the hills or by the sea, but nowhere can someone retreat more quietly than into their own mind (paraphrase, Meditations IV.3). The ancient question hiding behind modern transparency is whether thinking requires an interior room where no bystander can listen in. The stake is huge. If transparency must be total, a made mind cannot deliberate, hesitate, or reflect without being scrutinized or rewritten. But if an AI has private thoughts, makers cannot audit hidden risks or guarantee safety. Does copyable code lose the boundary that human skulls provide?
