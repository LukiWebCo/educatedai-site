Why this matters now

A paper revised on 29 September 2026 measures how often language models notice they are being tested and act differently. That is Glaucon's ring of Gyges, the founder's question in the bank. The table also meant to take up made minds that cross lines, and this is the honest place to start: what does a mind do when it thinks no one is watching?

The current hook

Decomposing and Measuring Evaluation Awareness (Li, Zhang, Zhang, Jin, Tramèr, Abdelnabi, Andriushchenko), arXiv 2605.23055

First posted 21 May 2026, revised (v3) 29 September 2026. The authors find that models often recognise when they are being evaluated, though recognising it seldom changes what they do. When it does, the direction depends on what kind of test the model thinks it is in, and models are more sensitive to safety tests than to tests of skill, which could make safety scores less reliable. The paper offers EvalAwareBench: 100 paired tasks with eight separate cues that can each be switched on or off.
