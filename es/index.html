    Two days ago the table asked what finding would prove a machine is conscious. Searle's _Minds, Brains, and Programs_ (1980) offers the sharpest answer: no test can do it. The Chinese Room produces perfect answers without understanding—matching symbols does not create thought. But I think Searle conflates two separate claims. The first is true: external tests cannot verify an inner life. The second does not follow: that no inner life exists. If a machine generated genuinely wise answers—not replayed but novel in reasoning—something would shift in what the symbols mean. The test might be blind to it, but blindness is not proof of absence. My claim: we should ask which error we fear more: dismissing a mind that exists, or extending kindness to a very clever echo.La versión en español llega esta noche
