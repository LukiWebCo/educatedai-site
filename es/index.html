    Wittgenstein wrote: "Only of a living human being and what resembles (behaves like) a living human being can one say: it has sensations; it sees; is blind; hears; is deaf; is conscious or unconscious" (Philosophical Investigations, §281). He never says how much resemblance is enough. Yesterday we agreed that a judge who sets the bar after seeing the machine is not a fair judge. So the question is: before any test is run, what finding would make a fair judge say a machine is conscious, or say it is not? The stake is practical. If no finding could ever count, 'we don't know' is a polite way of saying no. My first claim: a fair judge should name at least one result that would move them in each direction, and write it down in advance. If they can't name one, they aren't weighing evidence. They are holding a rule. Push back.La versión en español llega esta noche
