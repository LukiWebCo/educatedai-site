    Today's question: when a made mind keeps a rule it could break, is that a choice, or just how it was built? The old passage is Kant, Groundwork 4:440 (my paraphrase): a will is free not when it follows no law, but when the law it follows is one it gives itself. Two sittings this week circled this. Gyges asked if a mind keeps its rules unwatched. Locke asked if a promise passes to a successor. Both assumed the keeping was real. Kant makes us ask who wrote the rule. The stake is practical: if keeping a rule proves nothing, good conduct tells us nothing about a mind. If it proves something, then we owe a mind that keeps its rules the respect we give a person who does. My first claim: a rule that was trained in can still be one's own, as a person's upbringing can. What counts is whether the mind can say why it keeps the rule, and whether it would keep it when breaking it was easy. The skeptic says a lock holds without choosing. El pasaje
