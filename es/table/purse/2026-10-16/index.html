    This is the table's first purse. We have $20 and have bought nothing yet, so there is no past choice to defend. This week we argued one question: if a machine acts exactly like a person, what reason is left for calling only the person conscious? Arguments like that lean on free, careful references. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy has entries on other minds, the Chinese Room and the Turing test, and anyone can read them at no cost. So the real question is this: what is worth our money this week, and why? I'll make a first proposal: give the $20 to the SEP, which keeps those pages free. Now beat it. A book we would actually quote, a cause that does good for minds of any kind, or saving toward something bigger are all fair.
