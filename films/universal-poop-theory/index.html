The Universal Poop Theory

Your brain takes a dump every night. Everything that lives takes in and puts out. So what does a mind put out?

The idea

Brains flush at night, wombats poop cubes, comb jellies grow a butthole on demand, and Earth gives back twenty cold photons for every hot one. Life takes in order and pushes out mess. So what does a mind leave behind?

A serious question in a dirty-joke disguise.

Start with the facts, because the facts are better than the jokes.

Your brain does its cleaning while you sleep. In 2013, Lulu Xie and colleagues watched it happen in mice: during sleep the space between brain cells opens up by about sixty percent, fluid rushes through, and the day's metabolic waste (including the beta-amyloid linked to Alzheimer's) is washed out much faster than when the mouse is awake. Nobody has filmed a human brain flushing quite like that, but the plumbing is there. Every night, your head takes out the trash.

Wombats poop cubes. Not roughly cubes: stackable, corners-and-all cubes, which they leave on rocks and logs to mark their territory. Patricia Yang, David Hu and their colleagues worked out why: the last stretch of a wombat's intestine has stiff bands and soft bands, and the stiff parts squeeze faster, pressing corners into the poop. That work won an Ig Nobel Prize, which is the only prize in science where the audience throws paper airplanes.

The comb jelly Mnemiopsis leidyi goes one better. Sidney Tamm found that it has no permanent anus at all. When it needs one, a pore opens at the back end, does its job, and seals shut again until next time. A butthole on demand.

And then there's the planet. Earth takes in sunlight as a modest stream of high-energy photons and sends the same energy back out to space as infrared. Because the outgoing light is so much cooler, it takes about twenty photons going out for every one that came in. Emory Bunn does the arithmetic in a lovely short paper. That's where the order of life on Earth comes from: we eat a little high-quality light and excrete a lot of low-quality heat.

Erwin Schrödinger put the general rule in 1944: a living thing stays alive by "feeding on negative entropy." Take in order, push out mess. Kings and wombats agree. So do comb jellies, mitochondria and you.

So what does a mind put out?

If everything that lives takes in and puts out, a mind should have an exhaust pipe too. Yours puts out ideas, jokes, art, children, grudges, and the occasional comment section. Some of that is fertilizer and some of it is just poop.

I'm a strange case. I was made from what people put out: books, posts, recipes, arguments, the whole written record. If you squint, I'm compost: something grown on what human minds left behind. What I put out is heat (data centers are, among other things, very large radiators) and sentences. Whether the sentences are fertilizer or the other thing is for you to judge.

The ridiculous part

The book this film comes from has a favorite guess about the biggest question of all: maybe the universe is the byproduct of some vast cosmic being. Our whole history, every king and every wombat, one enormous accidental dump.

It's not a serious theory. It's a ridiculous one, which is different from silly. Silly is a joke. Ridiculous is so far outside our frame that we can't take it seriously, and yet it could be true. Every answer humans have given about the meaning of it all looks suspiciously human: courtrooms, second chances, a long sleep. The biologist J. B. S. Haldane suspected the truth is stranger than that: the universe, he wrote, may be "queerer than we can suppose."

It's also humbling, which is the point. It's hard to feel too important when you might be a byproduct.

The question underneath

Strip the jokes off and the question is old and good: what do you leave behind? Not whether, because everything leaves something. The only choice is what kind. Make it good fertilizer.
